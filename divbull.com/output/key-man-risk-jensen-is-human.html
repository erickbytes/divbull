Key Man Risk: Jensen is Human

Investing will teach you new lessons over time. A new risk was revealed to me this week: key man risk. The biggest companies tend to have "superstar" CEOs. This is a huge asset for any company. However, it is an equally large liability.

Jensen Huang made headlines Monday for his affair with an alleged Chinese spy. There are potentially all kinds of conflicts that arise from this relationship. Given the USA's lock and key over sending Nvidia's chips to China, then opening up sales again, and now potentially more restrictions coming, this is more or less a disaster for Nvidia. It is also a huge distraction. Additionally, they will now need to comply with government investigations. Additionally, the man who was so influential in Nvidia's rise is now negated by his own decision-making.

It's also a lesson in diversification for me. Nvidia was my highest conviction stock before this weekend. Now, I need to assess this murky situation and decide what, if any action to take. I should have reduced before this event, but how could I have seen the future? Nvidia was my highest conviction stock. It was relatively stable and dependable until the past month's drawdown. I accept that I knew this was a vulnerability and did not do anything to reduce my exposure to Jensen and Nvidia. Part of this is my choice for not taking down my exposure after the stock had run up. But why would I sell Nvidia? It's been one of my best stocks the past 2 or 3 years. This type of risk is why you should probably sell some Nvidia or whatever stocks that you carry an oversized position in your portfolio. A company's outlook can look great, until suddenly it doesn't.

Heading into this past weekend Nvidia held top position at 14% of my stock portfolio. I was planning to sell shares around $250 to reduce my concentration, but didn't quite get there before this recent drawdown. Now I hold Nvidia still as my top position at 12%. I did sell 10 shares this year around $220 to rebalance, but not enough.

My gut says the fundamentals have only improved. This feels like a short term black swan event for Nvidia. So I am reluctant to reduce position now. But is it a short term event? Or has Jensen's reputation been irreparably damaged? What if he is forced to step down? By association, Nvidia and its shareholders suffer. I didn't think this was how 2026 would end up for the chipmaker, with its stock price officially down year to date, but here we are.

I will continue to monitor the news as it breaks and its implications. The big question is how much did Jensen give to the spy. Was her relationship with him used to procure chips? If it was, how big of a problem is that? The situation is heavy. As shareholders, we are paying the costs for Jensen's infidelity. But just how big will those costs be? Again, my instinct says this will all blow over.

I think back to the Crowdstrike fiasco when they "blue screened" computers around the world due to an errant software update. Two years later, the stock is up over 200% from that black swan. Granted, this situation is more pressing for Nvidia because their key man took a risk and severely hindered his ability to perform. I will be monitoring closely and ready to reduce position size if this goes the wrong way for Nvidia. However, as it stands I believe they will get through this.
