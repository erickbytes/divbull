Title: Defensive Minded Plays in My Investing Portfolio 
Date: 2026-11-18 4:20 
Category: Essay


**Gold (SGOL)**

I decided to start buying gold recently. The main motivation came from reading about "[The Weird Portfolio](https://valuestockgeek.medium.com/the-weird-portfolio-99c0154d1c4a)", which I highly recommend reading. Reading this blog post inspired me to think more about other allocating to other asset classes like "small-cap value" in addition to gold and real estate. Value Stock Geek's version of the weird portfolio allocates 25% to gold! My allocation is now about 1% to SGOL. I am thinking I might raise it as high as 5% over time. I made the decision to buy SGOL for extra geographic diversification. When I buy the ETF, I have my physical gold stored in a vault in Switzerland, not the United States.


**Real Estate Investment Trusts aka REITs (O)**

Buying REITs means you are sacrificing potentially higher returns in exchange for stability and less severe drawdowns compared to tech stocks.
I allocated more recently to Realty Income. It pays a monthly dividend. This holding is exclusivly held in my tax advantages accounts,
so I don't pay taxes on the dividend yield. Additionally, you could hold VNQ like value stock geek to hold a basket of REITs. I might
hold a fund like this someday, in addition to Realty Income.


**Utilities (XLU)**

Similar to REITS, you don't buy utilities expecting maximum return versus more growth-y stocks. This means less severe drawdowns also.
I allocated a small position to utilities because they could be a long term winner in the AI buildout. Buying utilities means owning the 
electric power grid, a key component to humanity's future.


**Short Term Treasury Bonds aka "T-Bills" (SGOV)**

I use SGOV to earn 3.88%  on my cash holdings. Cash is a position. It is necessary to keep to maintain liquidity in your life.
It also enables you to be timely in seizing opportunities in the stock market.


**Value Stocks (VBR, IJS)**

Value stocks are outperforming growth in 2026. allocated to VBR and IJS, value stock funds. Value stocks are doing better than the Mag 7 this year. It won't be like that every year.


**Dividend Stocks (TM, KO, SCHD)**

I have some smaller dividend stocks holdings like Coca-Cola and Toyota. Coca-cola trades at an swollen multiple, but the business is unflappable and continues to execute its playbook. Toyota was hit hard by tariffs. It has an interesting long term vision for its business as a robotics leader.
In the meantime, they'll continue selling a ton of hybrid cars. I don't hold SCHD, the Charles Schwab dividend ETF currently, but it's another good option to hold a basket of dividend stocks.


**Consumer Staples (COST)**

Costco trades at a shy-high valuation. It's an incredible business. It is in the staples sector, which tends
to perform better in tougher economic times. Rain or shine, people are going to Costco and will love it.


**S&P 500 (SWPPX and VFIAX)**

More often than not, I would have been better off just buying the market versus stockpicking. 
Although the S&P index is capable of steep drawdowns, it is certainly more defensive in nature than 
picking the winners of the future. However, don't we wouls be wise to include some international 
exposure in the portfolio.
